### 1. Shortcut Pricing & Plans

Shortcut offers four subscription tiers:

*   **Free** — $0 per user/month for teams of up to 10 people. It includes Kanban boards, roadmaps, reports, sprints, docs, and integrations with GitHub, Slack, and Figma. [(shortcut.com/pricing)](https://www.shortcut.com/pricing)
*   **Team** — $8.50 per user/month when billed annually (or $10 monthly). This adds unlimited users, multiple customizable workflows (up to five), work-in-progress limits, automations, and access to all integrations. [(shortcut.com/faq)](https://www.shortcut.com/faq)
*   **Business** — $12 per user/month when billed annually (or $16 monthly). It includes unlimited everything, objectives/OKR tracking, advanced custom fields, advanced reporting, and multiple workspaces. [(shortcut.com/faq)](https://www.shortcut.com/faq)
*   **Enterprise** — Custom pricing. Adds single sign-on (SSO) and enterprise-grade support. [(shortcut.com/pricing)](https://www.shortcut.com/pricing)

### 3. Recent Reception (2024–2025)

Overall, Shortcut receives solid marks for ease of use and agile workflows, though some users note integration gaps and lighter enterprise analytics.

*   **Aggregate Ratings:** It holds a **4.4/5** on G2 (from 177+ reviews) and a **4.6/5** on Software Advice/Capterra (from 363 reviews). [(shortcut.com/pricing)](https://www.shortcut.com/pricing) [(softwareadvice.com)](https://www.softwareadvice.com/project-management/clubhouse-profile) [(capterra.com)](https://www.capterra.com/p/160498/Shortcut)
*   **Gartner Peer Insights:** Reviews are mixed-to-positive. One Principal Software Engineer rated it **3.0/5**, calling it a “solid, customizable, cost-effective choice” but noting weaker third-party integrations (e.g., Datadog) compared to larger platforms, which creates “toil” when trying to build consistent views across tools. [(gartner.com)](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/product/shortcut)
*   **Hands-on User Feedback:** After six months of use, a reviewer praised Shortcut for balancing “complexity with simplicity,” with an intuitive interface that gets teams started without days of training. Criticisms include reporting that is “solid but not especially detailed” and fewer advanced customization options than Jira. The reviewer concluded it is best for startups and development teams, and that some features feel unnecessary for general business project management. [(YouTube review)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YcBqQfD0s34)
*   **Sentiment Analysis (RFP.wiki):** Positive themes include speed/simplicity versus “heavyweight agile suites,” strong Git and Slack integrations, and dependable story/epic workflows. Recurring negatives include limited cross-project portfolio views and lighter dependency modeling compared to top enterprise suites. [(rfp.wiki)](https://www.rfp.wiki/project-management/collaborative-work-management/shortcut-software/linear)

### Summary

**Shortcut** competes as a dev-friendly alternative to Jira that is cheaper and faster to set up, while offering more engineering structure than Asana, Linear, or generalist tools. Its annual paid plans ($8.50–$12/user) undercut Asana and Linear, but prospective buyers should verify that their required third-party integrations are supported, and that they do not need Jira-grade reporting or dependency mapping.
