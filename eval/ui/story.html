Project story · PM tools purchase evaluation

Search product comparison

A search agent writes a confident brief about PM tool prices. We built a checker, measured it against hand-labelled claims, and used the results to recommend what to buy.

8
PM tools checked: Linear, Jira, Plane, Shortcut, ClickUp, Asana, Trello, YouTrack

The problem

A PM choosing a tool needs facts that are current, cited, and checkable: the price, the billing unit, whether SSO is included, and whether a claim holds up against its source. A search-plus-LLM agent produces a fluent brief quickly, but some of its claims do not match the pages it cites.

What we built

Evaluator

Plans its searches, drops off-topic results, reads the full text of each page, and checks every price against its source before writing the brief. A claim it cannot verify is labelled "not yet verified."

Purchase recommender

Reads verified prices, verified SSO facts, and a PM memory file (budget and must-have features). It returns renew, negotiate, review alternatives, or insufficient data, with a confidence score and the factors behind it.

What the starter code produced, and what ours produced

The starter code answers a question with one search and one model reply. Its answer is free text with links scattered through it. Our code writes a structured brief: each claim carries source IDs, claims without a valid source are separated out, and each price is checked against its cited page before the brief is written.

How we measured it

We labelled 90 pricing and feature claims by hand against their pages. The labelling rule: a partial match counts as not stated. We then scored an LLM judge against those labels.

Why the enhancements matter to the business

  • Every claim traces to a source. A buyer or procurement reviewer can click through to the page behind each price or SSO claim, and see its date. This makes a recommendation defensible in a review.
  • Wrong prices stay out of the history. Only prices with a unit, billing period, and a supporting quote are stored. A bad number cannot quietly drive a renewal decision.
  • Known gaps are visible. The recommender reports "insufficient data" when a price is missing, instead of guessing. A team can see what to check before it spends money.
  • Judgments are measured, not assumed. The judge's agreement with hand labels is recorded, including where it is weaker. That tells the business how far to trust the tool before it replaces manual research.
  • Memory sets the rules. Budget and must-have features live in one editable file, so the recommendation follows the team's policy, not a model's preference.
  • Cost of a wrong call. Renewing a tool that lacks a required feature, or paying more than the budget, costs more than the time spent on these checks. The roadmap items are priced by that risk.
